# AI tools in market research: trust failure modes (2025 wave)

## Sample and method

A mixed-method study run by an independent insights consortium in Q3 2025. Sample: 217 senior insights professionals across agencies and in-house corporate teams in India, the UK, Singapore, and the United States. Quant phase: 217 online survey responses; qual phase: 24 in-depth interviews drawn from the survey sample.

The objective was to map *trust failure modes* — the specific moments where AI tools lose researcher trust — and to identify which guardrails meaningfully shift adoption.

## Top finding: hallucination is the dominant trust killer

73% of senior researchers cited fabricated statistics as the single most damaging failure mode. The phrase "fireable offence" appeared in 14 of the 24 qualitative interviews unprompted. A senior partner at a Mumbai-based agency stated: "A fabricated statistic in a client deck is a fireable offence — for the analyst, sometimes for the partner who signed off."

Other ranked trust failures:
- **Lack of project memory**: 62% cited that general-purpose tools have no knowledge of prior client work, making them structurally unsuitable for longitudinal or agency contexts.
- **Generic outputs**: 54% reported that AI tools default to "ChatGPT-flavoured platitudes" that any junior can produce.
- **Poor citation discipline**: 48% cited the inability to verify claims against source documents as a Day-1 disqualifier.

## Provider-specific behaviour

The study ran a controlled set of 60 test prompts across leading AI providers. Two providers stood out for opposite reasons:

- **Anthropic Claude** was flagged by reviewers as cautious-by-default. In 4 of 60 test prompts, Claude returned an explicit "I cannot verify this from the available context" response rather than fabricating. Reviewers rated this behaviour as a positive trust signal.
- **OpenAI GPT-4** produced confident-sounding fabrications in 41% of test prompts when asked questions where the underlying context was insufficient. Reviewers rated this as the single largest barrier to enterprise adoption.

## What guardrails actually shift adoption

The qualitative phase identified three guardrails that meaningfully changed researcher willingness to adopt:

1. **Structured-output schemas with mandatory citation fields**: outperformed prompt-only "please cite your sources" approaches in 22 of 24 qualitative interviews.
2. **Verifier passes**: a second-model fact-check that drops unsupported claims rather than rewriting them. Cited as the difference between a "demo" and a "shippable system."
3. **Honest abstention**: explicit "the corpus does not address this" responses rated higher than confident-but-wrong responses on every dimension tested.

## Caveats

The sample skews toward agency researchers (61%) over in-house teams (39%). The Tier-3 and rural Indian context is not represented in this wave. The consortium recommends a follow-up quantitative wave with stronger geographic stratification.
