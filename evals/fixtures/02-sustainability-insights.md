# Sustainability purchase drivers: Tier-1 Gen-Z women (2024 longitudinal)

## Sample and method

A longitudinal panel study run across three waves in 2024. Sample: 412 women, aged 18 to 26, residing in Tier-1 metros in India (Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Pune). Method: online quant survey paired with two qualitative cohorts (n=18 each).

The objective was to measure the gap between stated and revealed preference on sustainability across grocery, personal care, and apparel.

## Headline finding

Sustainability ranks **#3** as a stated purchase driver, behind price (#1) and convenience (#2). On a 7-point importance scale, sustainability scored a stated average of **6.2/7** — high enough to suggest universal endorsement, low enough to be flagged for social desirability bias.

In behavioural choice tasks (forced-choice scenarios with a ₹15+ price premium for the sustainable option), only **23% of respondents** chose the sustainable product. The 6.2/7 stated importance and the 23% behavioural rate together represent a textbook attitude-behaviour gap.

## The Premium Loyalists segment

A small but distinctive segment, **12% of the sample**, chose the sustainable option consistently across price points up to a ₹50 premium. This segment was disproportionately:
- Working professionals aged 24-26 (vs. students)
- Mumbai or Bengaluru-based (vs. other Tier-1 metros)
- Self-reporting household income above ₹15 lakh per annum

The qualitative cohort interviews suggested this segment treats sustainability as a brand-tier signalling cue rather than a category attribute.

## Channel and category effects

- **Personal care**: sustainability premium tolerance was highest (revealed conversion at 31%).
- **Groceries**: lowest (revealed conversion at 14%).
- **Apparel**: middle (revealed conversion at 27%), with strong skew toward visible-sustainability (recyclable packaging, certified-organic labels) over invisible practices (supply chain, water use).

## Caveats and undersampled segments

The sample is **deliberately scoped to Tier-1 metros**. Tier-2 cities are partially represented (n=43, marked as exploratory). **Tier-3 cities and rural communities are not represented in this wave.** A follow-up wave with stronger Tier-3+ stratification was recommended in the close-out report and is scheduled for 2026.

Men are not part of this sample (women-only by design — the brief was specific to female-led purchase decisions in these categories).

The behavioural-choice scenarios used hypothetical product cards, not in-store observation. Real-world checkout behaviour may diverge further from stated preference.
