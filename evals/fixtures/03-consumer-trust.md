# Brand trust drivers across urban Indian categories (2026 syndicated study)

## Sample and method

Syndicated tracking study, Q1 2026 wave. Sample: 1,200 respondents across 8 urban Indian markets (Tier-1 and select Tier-2 cities), balanced by age (18-55) and gender (50/50). Method: 22-minute online panel survey with embedded conjoint and forced-choice modules.

## Top brand trust drivers, ranked

Across the 18 categories tested, the consolidated drivers of brand trust ranked:

1. **Heritage / longevity**: 67% of Tier-1 metro consumers prefer brands older than 25 years in personal care and packaged foods. The "born-after-2010 brand" penalty is most pronounced in oral care and skincare.
2. **Origin transparency**: 58% prioritise "Made in India" over imported equivalents at parity pricing in apparel, packaged foods, and small appliances.
3. **Influencer-driven trust**: 31% of Gen-Z (18-24) cite influencers as a top-3 trust input vs. 14% of Millennials (25-40) and 4% of Gen-X (41-55).
4. **Expert endorsement**: 44% across all age cohorts cite a doctor / dietitian / industry expert recommendation as a positive trust signal.
5. **Peer recommendation**: 52% across all cohorts; highest in groceries and personal care.

## Named segments

The study identified four named consumer segments via cluster analysis on the trust-driver battery:

- **Pragmatic Tier-2 Switchers** (28% of sample): price-led, low brand loyalty, high promotion responsiveness.
- **Premium Urban Heritage Loyalists** (19%): high heritage-preference, willing to pay 15-20% premium, low promotion responsiveness.
- **Origin-First Buyers** (22%): strong "Made in India" preference, moderate price elasticity, high in apparel and packaged foods.
- **Experimental Gen-Z Adopters** (15%): low heritage-preference, high influencer-driven, high category-switching frequency.

The remaining 16% of the sample did not cluster cleanly and were classified as "Mixed motivations."

## Caveats and undersampled segments

The methodology is online-panel-based, which under-represents:

- **Rural communities** (zero coverage in this wave; rural respondents make up roughly 65% of India's adult population)
- **Tier-3 cities and below** (limited to 8% of sample; under-represented relative to true population share)
- **Lower-income urban communities** (HHI under ₹4 lakh per annum is at 12% of sample vs. estimated 35% population share in the panelled cities)

These exclusions are documented in the study's methodology appendix. Findings should be read as **applicable to the urban, panel-accessible Tier-1 and Tier-2 Indian consumer**, not the Indian consumer in general.
